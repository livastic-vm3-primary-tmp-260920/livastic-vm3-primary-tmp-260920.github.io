Pilot acceptance scope, subject to agreement

What the merchant would receive

  1. Input contract: at most 50 invoice rows (sanitized references, expected USDC amounts and merchant-supplied attribution references) plus at most 50 seller-authorized candidate token Transfer events, with explicitly stated covered date range and address/network. Customer decides what to disclose.
  2. Matched/unmatched CSV: classifications, fixed-point decimal discrepancies, duplicate source keys (chain ID + transaction hash + log index), and documented ambiguous/partial matches that need human attention.
  3. Evidence/uncertainty memo: public explorer references only for genuine independently verifiable receipts, token contract, recipient, chain ID and number of confirmations as observed at agreed snapshot. A network outage or unsupported chain yields an explicit unverified state, not an invented receipt.
  4. Review handoff: one plain-English/Markdown exception report and a merchant review checklist. No automatic reconciliation, ERP integration, tax classification, payment forwarding, wallet signing or change to merchant systems.
What this cannot know by itself: An ERC-20 Transfer log does not carry an invoice ID. Without merchant-approved attribution, several identical amounts are ambiguous. A receipt missing from a partial export is not proof of nonpayment. Multiple token Transfer logs may share one txHash; each genuine logIndex matters. This is not a security audit, accounting opinion, tax advice, chargeback service, legal attestation, or a guaranteed fraud finding.
